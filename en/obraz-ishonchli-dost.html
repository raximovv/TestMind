How this character is derived

This character comes from your two strongest traits: Emotional stability and Agreeableness. You have the other three as well — these two simply scored higher.

If those two traits score very closely together, the test shows you the second character too. The result is a snapshot of today, not a fixed label.

At home At home and with the people close to you

Your strengths

  • Peace at home When an argument starts you do not raise your voice. Usually you are the first person to soften it.
  • Trusted first Your brother or sister tells you about a problem before anyone else, because they know it will not reach the adults.
  • Help without being asked If you can see someone is tired you do not wait to be asked. The person who washed up is usually you.
  • Patience with older people You listen to your grandmother tell the same story a third time without getting bored. Not many people can.
  • A promise at home What you promised your family, you remember. Even a small thing.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Hiding what you need Saying “I do not need anything” often enough, you stop asking even when you really do.
  • Changing the subject Instead of settling a disagreement you steer the talk elsewhere. It does not go away — it collects.
  • Carrying everyone else “It is easier if I do it” — so you do the housework yourself, and then cannot work out why you are tired.
  • Not showing you are hurt When something stings you go quiet. Your family does not know why, and it looks as though they have missed something.
  • Hard to say no You do what is asked even when it breaks your own plans. Afterwards it feels tight, because no time was left for you.

At school In lessons, in exams and among classmates

Your strengths

  • Holding group work up The part nobody took on is the part you finish at the end.
  • Classmates trust you Even the student who gets on with nobody opens up to you, because you do not judge.
  • Calm in an exam While everyone else is nervous you work at your usual speed. And you do not forget what you knew.
  • How teachers see you You do not push back and you keep your word, so the responsible job often comes to you.
  • Slowly, but without stopping Not in one night, but a little every day. That is how the result adds up by the end of the year.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Not asking questions Asking about the part you did not follow feels awkward, as if you would hold the class up. Then that topic gets harder.
  • Underrating yourself You judge your work to be weaker than a classmate's. Most of the time that is not true.
  • Standing back from leading You hand the group leader's job to someone else, even when the team chose you.
  • Giving way in an argument If someone speaks with a firm tone you do not defend even a right answer.
  • Doing it instead of helping Instead of explaining, you do it yourself. They learn nothing and you work twice.

In relationships In friendship and close relationships

Your strengths

  • A real listener You are not preparing your answer — you are actually listening. People notice at once.
  • A secret stays with you What is said to you goes no further. Your friends have tested that.
  • You make peace When two friends fall out, the one who talks to both is usually you.
  • Friendship that lasts Your friendships run for years. You are not the one who lets them go.
  • No judgement Even someone who got it wrong is not afraid to tell you. That is why they tell you.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Being taken advantage of Someone who knows how kind you are can use it — and you are usually the last to notice.
  • No boundary Your phone is open at midnight too. No time is left over for you.
  • Storing up hurt You say nothing about what stung, then suddenly pull away. Your friend does not know why.
  • Wanting to please everyone You keep your view to yourself and agree with the majority. Your view was needed too.
  • Leaving yourself no time You give your friends so much that being alone feels like a fault. Rest is part of it as well.

Directions that might suit you

  • Psychology and counselling82%

    People tell you their own things and you listen without cutting in. Staying calm under pressure is required here too.

  • Speech therapy and special education77%

    You do not give up where nothing shows for months. Patience matters more than talent here.

  • Social work and HR77%

    You feel tension in a group before anyone else and you can settle people. Here you will also have to say what you think.

  • Medicine and nursing75%

    An emergency does not throw you and you treat a patient as a person. Getting used to strict procedure will take more work.

  • Teaching and childcare70%

    A child who did not understand gets your third explanation as clearly as the first. But speaking in front of a class will be the hard part.

These percentages show how well your two strongest sides fit each direction. They are not a prediction: your interest, your opportunities and your real experience matter more.
